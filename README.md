# trenching-bot

Read-only Solana paper-trading research system. The deterministic fixture slice is implemented: wallet balance deltas -> the dip-entry strategy (the tracked wallet's first buy of each token when it is valued above a $10,000 market cap, a 15% dip from it, then +30% take profit or -15% stop loss, all within about 30 minutes) -> modeled fills -> atomic PostgreSQL persistence and JSON report. Live wallet/market ingestion remains pending. No transactions are constructed, signed or sent.

The roadmap is [WEEKLY_EXECUTION_PLAN](docs/WEEKLY_EXECUTION_PLAN.md). See [implementation status](docs/IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS.md), [the dip-entry strategy](docs/adr/20261008-dip-entry-strategy.md), [original fixture semantics](docs/adr/0001-fixture-simulation-semantics.md), [fixture format](docs/REPLAY_FORMAT.md), and [provider research/setup](docs/PROVIDER_SPIKE.md).

## Stack and requirements

Kotlin 2.3.21, Java 21, Spring Boot 4.1.1, Gradle wrapper 9.6.0, PostgreSQL 17, JPA, MapStruct 1.6.3/kapt and JUnit/Testcontainers. MapStruct generates the persistence DTO mappings; replay and the RPC smoke test use the existing runtime libraries.

Set `JAVA_HOME` to an installed JDK 21. Docker is required for PostgreSQL integration tests and the Spring application, but not for offline tests or standalone replay. On Windows use `.\gradlew.bat`; on Unix use `./gradlew`.

The run totals -0.013487824000 SOL over 10 cycles (5 entered, 2 closed). The [dip-entry decision](docs/adr/20261008-dip-entry-strategy.md) records every rule. In short:
- one cycle per token starts at the tracked wallet's first confirmed signer buy of it, at that buy's price P0;
- the buy must be valued above $10,000 market cap (P0 × 1B supply × the dataset's SOL/USDT rate for its minute), otherwise the cycle ends as `REFERENCE_BELOW_MIN_MARKET_CAP` ([decision](docs/adr/20261023-reference-market-cap.md), engine `dip-paper-v3`);
- the first eligible trade in a later slot at or below P0 × 0.85 and before the bonding curve completes triggers the entry, whatever its own market cap;
- entries and exits fill at the first eligible trade at least `latencySlots` (default 1) after their trigger;
- take profit is at Pe × 1.30 and stop loss at Pe × 0.85, where Pe is the entry's curve execution price (our own price impact included, fees excluded);
- a take profit or stop loss sells into that trade only if it lands within 60 s of the trigger (spec Section 8's active phase, by block time, inclusive); otherwise it sells against the curve standing at `trigger slot + latencySlots` (the post-trade curve of the last eligible trade before it, `STANDING_CURVE`), once the recording proves no trade landed within the 60 s (a later trade, or the window's recorded end time), and stays open as `HORIZON_REACHED` until then; when no curve can pay, the position stays open as `UNRESOLVED`;
- everything happens within 4,500 slots of the buy.

The timed clocks are optional and counted from block times, never slot counts ([decision](docs/adr/20261024-timed-dip-window-and-exit.md), engine `dip-paper-v4`); with none set a run behaves as above. `dipWindowMs` lets only a trade at most that long after the buy's block time `t0` trigger, inclusive; the first later trade ends the watch as `NO_DIP`. `maxHoldMs` and `hardExitStartMs`, set together, add a third exit, `TIME_EXIT`, at `exitStart = min(fill time + maxHoldMs, t0 + hardExitStartMs)`: take profit and stop loss are only checked on trades before it, and the position is then sold whole against the curve standing at `exitStart`, once the recording proves it covers that moment (a later trade, or the window's recorded end time); otherwise it stays open as `HORIZON_REACHED`. The agreed preset sets them to 30 s, 3 min and 4 min.

Two more rules are optional and off by default, so a run with neither behaves as above ([decision](docs/adr/20261025-post-trade-price-and-wallet-sell.md), engine `dip-paper-v5`; with both off its outcomes equal `dip-paper-v4`'s). `priceMeasure` (`TRADE_AVERAGE` by default, or `POST_TRADE`) picks the trade price the reference, the minimum market cap, the dip and the take-profit and stop-loss triggers compare; the exit levels still come from our own average fill. Under `POST_TRADE` each comparison uses the curve's spot price right after the trade, and the reference is the spot price after the wallet's buy, read from that buy's own market observation (same pair, slot, signature and instruction, and the wallet as buyer when the trade is recorded), which must be an eligible price. Without exactly one such eligible observation the cycle ends as `REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE`, with no fallback to the buy's average; captures from before the 50-slot market window lead never contain the buy, so every cycle of theirs ends that way. `followWalletSells` (`false` by default) reads the tracked wallet's own sells of the mint from the market data: a sell from the buy's slot through the dip trigger's slot, the trigger's own slot included, ends the cycle with no entry as `WALLET_SOLD`; a sell after the trigger while we hold (or before our fill lands) exits 100% of the position as `WALLET_SOLD`, whatever amount the wallet sold, sold like a take profit or stop loss. A sell at or after a timed position's `exitStart` changes nothing. The rule needs observations that record each trade's trader and side (schema v4 or later): a run asking for it on a v3 dataset is refused with `SELL_CHECK_NEEDS_TRADES` before anything runs or is stored. The agreed preset sets `POST_TRADE` and `true`.

A minimum signal size (spec P3) is optional too ([decision](docs/adr/20261102-fee-inclusive-signal-size.md), engine `dip-paper-v6`; unset, a run's outcomes equal `dip-paper-v5`'s). `minSignalSol` makes the tracked wallet's buy a signal only when it paid strictly more, fees included: its curve payment plus the protocol and creator fees on it at the buy's own basis points, each rounded up to a whole lamport, all in whole lamports. Both are read from the buy's own market observation, the one `POST_TRADE` reads its reference from. A buy paying at most the minimum ends the cycle as `SIGNAL_BELOW_MIN_SIZE` with no entry; without exactly one eligible observation recording the buy's trade the cycle ends as `SIGNAL_SIZE_UNAVAILABLE`, with no fallback to the wallet's own transaction. The size is checked after an unsupported buy and an unavailable post-trade reference, and before the minimum market cap. It needs observations that record trades (schema v4 or later): a run asking for it on a v3 dataset is refused with `SIGNAL_SIZE_NEEDS_TRADES` before anything runs or is stored. The agreed preset sets 1.9 SOL, so agreed run ids, qualification verdicts and `DECIDED` capture cutoffs change from `dip-paper-v6` on.

Execution follows the [bonding-curve execution decision](docs/adr/20261009-bonding-curve-execution.md) (engine `dip-paper-v2`). Each entry spends 0.1 SOL (a whole number of lamports) against the fill trade's post-trade Pump.fun curve: tokens out and SOL out use the program's integer formula rounding down, so our own price impact follows from the curve, and an entry larger than the curve's real token reserves is rejected as `EXCEEDS_CURVE`. The protocol and creator fees are the fill trade's own basis points, rounded up to whole lamports, paid on top of the notional on entry and out of the proceeds on exit. A fixed `transactionCostSol` (default 0.001 SOL, for the network fee plus priority fee or tip) is charged on each entry and each exit; token-account rent is not charged, since it is refunded when the account closes. Notional, latency and transaction cost are editable per run. Prices must be at most two slots old by price slot. Quote amounts and quantities use scale 12; prices keep scale 38 so memecoin prices stay exact; both round HALF_EVEN. Only swaps against the configured quote mint (wrapped SOL by default) become trades; others are ignored as `UNSUPPORTED_QUOTE`. Every cycle that never enters and every position that never exits carries an explicit reason.

Repeat runs produce identical IDs and result checksums; duplicate input delivery is deduplicated and conflicting identities fail. Persisted replay stores the complete lifecycle in one transaction. Repeating it after a process restart returns the original run and does not create new records. Different dataset/configuration/engine versions have independent run identities. A stored run keeps its inputs in one of two ways ([decision](docs/adr/20261102-dataset-referenced-runs.md)): `COPY`, the default and the only storage the console's `POST /api/runs` uses, stores the dataset snapshot and its market observations under the run; `DATASET_REFERENCE`, for automated sweep runs, stores neither and reads them from the catalog dataset, which then cannot be deleted. Storage never changes a run's id or result, and a repeat start returns the run as first stored. The bundled sample is a synthetic fixture, not verified Decu transactions, and its route-supported flag is curated. A real dataset can be exported from recorded captures (below). Offline decoding of real captures supports only SOL-quoted Pump.fun bonding-curve buys (`buy` and `buy_exact_quote_in_v3`) signed by the tracked wallet ([decision](docs/adr/20261003-pumpfun-bonding-curve-buy-decoder.md), [V3 amendment](docs/adr/20261010-pumpfun-exact-quote-in-v3-buys.md)). Actual token metadata and live quote-asset restrictions are not implemented.

## Modules

Feature packages remain one deployable modular monolith. Shared value types live in `common`; domain contracts contain no Spring, JPA or vendor types.

| Module | Implemented responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| `common` | Validated base58 wallet/mint/signature values, slots, unsigned raw token amounts and decimal policy |
| `chaincapture` | Vendor-neutral, read-only Solana JSON-RPC capture plumbing: guarded transport, mainnet check, signature-listing parsing, budgets and shared limits, hashed artifact store, manifest field readers, parsed-transaction and unsafe-text checks |
| `protocol.pumpfun` | Pump.fun bonding-curve instruction layout and reader for `buy` and `TradeEvent` instructions, without market or wallet rules |
| `walletactivity` | Curated balance-delta normalization into accepted or ignored outcomes |
| `marketdata` | Slot-aware observations, ordered catalog, fixture data source; mint-referenced market capture (`MarketHistoryCapture`) and offline Pump.fun `LAST_TRADE` extraction (`RecordedMarketHistory`) |
| `strategy` | The dip-entry strategy: first buy per token, P0, the dip trigger with market cap, and run configuration |
| `papertrading` | Latency-delayed fills after a dip trigger against the Pump.fun bonding curve, real fees, transaction cost and explicit rejections |
| `portfolio` | Position lifecycle, take-profit and stop-loss exits sold into the curve, and quote P&L |
| `replay` | Checksummed fixture loader, deterministic orchestration, offline/persisted commands, durable run ledger, capture commands and market-window planning from recorded wallet buys |
| `console` | Gated dataset/run REST API, local React console, dashboard and on-demand read-only Solana probe |

Implementation packages are `domain`, `application`, `infrastructure`, and `api`. `chaincapture` and `protocol.pumpfun` are the exception: flat packages of small building blocks with no Spring context, annotations or database (the transport uses spring-web's `RestClient`) that `walletactivity` and `marketdata` share, so neither feature imports the other's capture internals. Other modules consume public contracts/domain values. Notification/Telegram work is deferred; the earlier README's Telegram implementation description did not correspond to this checkout.

Each feature owns its entities, repositories, MapStruct mappings and public store port. Replay coordinates these ports; it does not import another feature's JPA types. Tables are `replay_datasets`, `simulation_runs`, `wallet_events`, `market_observations`, `trade_intents`, `paper_orders`, `paper_fills` and `positions`. Unique constraints, provenance foreign keys, position state checks, an open-scenario unique index and optimistic versions protect the lifecycle.

Model quote amounts and quantities use `numeric(38,12)`; execution and exit prices use `numeric(78,38)`; raw signed deltas use `numeric(20,0)` with u64 bounds; observed prices/liquidity use `numeric(78,38)` with original decimal scales retained. Unrepresentable decimals or source times finer than microseconds fail rather than silently round. Datasets (schema v3 to v6, with SOL/USDT minute rates and curve reserves, from v4 each observation's decoded trade, from v5 each market window's observed coverage, and from v6 the block time that coverage ends at; a run decided beyond that coverage is refused with `MARKET_COVERAGE_ENDED`, see [the format](docs/REPLAY_FORMAT.md#schema-v5-coverage) and [v6](docs/REPLAY_FORMAT.md#schema-v6-coverage-throughtime)) are bounded to 100,000 wallet transactions and 100,000 observations; intents, orders, fills and positions to 100,000 per run (at most one cycle per buy), so every loadable dataset persists. The loader applies the same timestamp, decimal and length checks as persistence. Run snapshots preserve inputs, configuration, engine/version, result checksum and model limitations. Creation time is UTC metadata, excluded from deterministic identity.

**Captures.** The local console can create a bounded wallet capture or attach existing evidence under the capture root, then run wallet, market and SOL/USDT-rate acquisition as background operations. It shows each invocation's allowance separately from cumulative usage. Wallet and market acquisition can opt into bounded automatic recovery with cumulative request, byte and attempt limits, provider retry delays, and pause/resume/cancel controls. Each wallet-planned market case is listed independently: downloaded counts reach `READY_TO_VALIDATE` before selected artifacts are verified, and a healthy case can then prepare its own rates, export a canonical schema-v6 dataset, download it or import it into Datasets while a sibling remains incomplete or invalid. The existing full-capture rates and export path stays strict and still requires every market window. Calibration also retains its full-capture checks. The **Wallet + token** scope takes one mint, verifies whether the wallet's earliest buy through its token account is its actual first Pump.fun curve buy, and qualifies the exported dataset automatically against the dip-entry strategy ([guide](docs/REAL_DATA_CAPTURE.md#wallet--token-capture), [independent-case decision](docs/adr/20261012-independent-market-cases.md)). Under the agreed configuration a wallet sell before the dip is a negative case, while a capture whose market data lacks the wallet's buy fails the replay as `REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE` and a v3 dataset as `SELL_CHECK_NEEDS_TRADES`, since neither can say whether the token qualified. Its market attempt defaults to `DECIDED` mode, which stops each window once its outcome under the agreed strategy's clocks is decided and the recording reaches at least 4 minutes after the wallet's buy ([decided and full mode](docs/REAL_DATA_CAPTURE.md#decided-and-full-mode)), while reference rates are enabled (`FULL` otherwise and for the other scopes); a `MARKET` operation with `cutoffMode` `FULL` captures the rest of a decided window, and `DECIDED` without reference rates is refused with `RATES_NOT_CONFIGURED`. The pages use the existing console theme and work in dark and light modes.

**Deleting.** `DELETE /api/captures/{id}` removes a capture session and every file the console created for it: managed evidence, exports and calibration reports. Attached directories are only referenced and are never touched, and imported datasets stay. A session with a running operation returns 409. `DELETE /api/datasets/{id}` removes a dataset from the catalog; its copy runs stay, each with its own copy of the dataset, and re-uploading the same file restores the same id. A dataset that dataset-referenced runs still read is not deleted: it returns 409 `DATASET_IN_USE`, with the number of those runs in the message, and nothing is removed. Both return 204, or 404 for an unknown id. Both work only from a local browser or client, never through the public preview tunnel (403 `LOCAL_ACCESS_REQUIRED`). The console asks for confirmation before either delete. See the [delete plan](docs/plans/archive/20261010-delete-captures-and-datasets.md).

After a backend restart, an eligible recovery-enabled wallet or market operation continues within its saved limits; paused, cancelled, stale or exhausted work stays stopped. An operation without recovery is marked interrupted and requires an explicit retry. Because a forced kill loses usage after the last checkpoint, recovery conservatively charges the remaining reserved allowance before retrying; artifacts written after the last manifest checkpoint can be fetched again. Capture routes are local-only and require the local profile with the console enabled. Configure SOLANA_RPC_URL in the backend process; the browser never receives the endpoint. Capture files default to build/captures and are removed by clean, so retained research should use an ignored root outside build or be backed up. The workflow stays read-only toward Solana; live trading, signing and transaction submission remain out of scope. **Automation.** The local console can watch one wallet (Decu) through the night and record its new Pump.fun buys with no clicks ([plan](docs/plans/archive/20261102-decu-capture-sweep-automation.md), [decision](docs/adr/20261102-automated-capture-and-sweep-evaluation.md)). It is off by default and loads only under the local profile with the console enabled. With `trenching.automation.capture-enabled=false` it runs **phase 0, discovery only**: it measures buys per night and the capture filter's yield before any capture spends provider requests. With `capture-enabled=true` it runs **phase 1, capture, sweep and scoring**: each buy is captured to a registered dataset, every configuration of the sweep plan is replayed on it, and each closed session scores every configuration over the last sessions. Results are flagged only; nothing is adopted as the default.
- **Discovery.** Every `discovery-interval` (10 min), inside the polling window (`poll-from` to `poll-to`, local times in `zone`; 18:00 to 05:00 UTC+5 by default, crossing midnight), it reads the wallet's activity newest first in pages of 20 (at most `max-discovery-pages`, 5) back to a per-wallet watermark stored in `automation_watermarks`. The first scan reaches back `initial-lookback` (12 h); it also stops at the first page whose oldest listed transaction is older than that, by the block time `getSignaturesForAddress` already returns, so a history with no decodable trade is not paged to its end. A scan cut short by the page limit stores what it read and keeps the watermark, and its row records a backlog cursor: `resume_before`, the oldest `nextBefore` read, where the next tick continues, and `pending_*`, the newest action of the whole catch-up. Once a scan reaches the watermark, the watermark advances to that newest action and the cursor is cleared, so a backlog of any size is reached; buys made meanwhile are read by the scan after. A first scan cut short has no watermark to reach yet: it stores its newest action as a provisional watermark at once (a placeholder before every action, slot `-1` and an empty signature, when its pages held no Pump.fun action, so the cursor is never lost) and keeps its lookback start in `resume_since` as the bound its catch-up still has to reach. `/status` shows the cursor as `watermark.resumeBefore`, and no watermark while the placeholder stands. The row's `updated_at` is the start of the last complete scan: one that read from the newest transaction down to its bound, or, when a later scan completes a catch-up, the scan from the newest transaction that began it, whose start the row keeps in `catch_up_started_at` meanwhile (during the first catch-up, the start of that catch-up). A scan covers every buy up to its start less a 60 s indexing margin, since the listing can show a transaction that late; that is what lets a session close. Outside the polling window discovery reads nothing, except while an open session whose last buy is before the window's most recent end (05:00 by default) has had its last buy plus `session-idle` pass (by the clock, less the margin) without a complete scan covering it: then it keeps scanning, one page or about 21 requests per tick, so the night's last session closes the same morning, and stops once no session waits. A session a daytime buy opened or extended never triggers a scan, so a wallet that keeps buying all day cannot keep the scans going; it waits for the window to open (18:00). The same holds for a pre-window session a morning scan extends with a buy just after 05:00: it waits for the evening. Repeats are absorbed by the unique keys. A rate-limited or unavailable provider skips the tick with a `WARN`; an unconfigured RPC or a non-mainnet endpoint does the same and shows on `/status`.
- **Filter.** A buy becomes an item when its curve payment, raised by `capture-fee-margin-bps` (300) for the fees the listing leaves out and rounded down to whole lamports, is strictly above `capture-min-signal-sol` (1.5 SOL). Sells, and later buys of a mint the wallet already has an item for, are ignored; buys at or below the filter during a session are counted as its `belowFilterCount`. A catch-up can find a mint's earlier buy after its later one: the earlier buy then replaces the later one's item while no capture step has touched it (`DISCOVERED` with no step, or `FAILED` with `MISSING_BLOCK_TIME`) and its session is still open, and that session is rebuilt from the items left (see Sessions). A `MISSING_BLOCK_TIME` item sets no span and takes no part in evaluation, so it is also replaced while its session is `CLOSING` (as when it opened a session of its own), and that session is deleted if it is left empty; a `CLOSED` session keeps it. Otherwise the item is kept and the earlier buy is logged at `WARN`, naming whether capture had started or the session had stopped taking buys, since the wallet + token capture proves the first buy anyway. While the wallet has a catch-up in progress, no item creates its capture session (`CREATE_SESSION` waits for a later tick), so the real first buy is found before capture starts. A buy without a block time is stored `FAILED` with `MISSING_BLOCK_TIME`; it joins the wallet's latest open session without changing its span, or, with none open, a session of its own at the discovery time that starts `CLOSING`, so it never takes or bridges a timed buy. Item and session ids are SHA-256 hashes of their input, so restarts converge.
- **Sessions.** A session is a run of buys with no gap longer than `session-idle` (60 min). A buy joins the open session it fits by time, from `session-idle` before its first buy to `session-idle` after its last, both ends included; otherwise it opens a new one. A catch-up reads buys older than the ones before it, so it can extend a session backwards or open an earlier session after a later one. A buy that fits two open sessions joins them into one: the session discovered first keeps its id (sessions opened by the same scan: the one with the earlier first buy, then the smaller id) and absorbs the other's items and sweep rows, moved in bulk. When an earlier buy replaces a later buy's item, the later buy's session is rebuilt from the items left: its span shrinks to theirs, it splits wherever two of them are now more than `session-idle` apart (the part holding its first item keeps it; each other part opens a session of its own, keyed by its earliest item, and takes its items' sweep rows), and a session left with no item is deleted; one left with only items without a block time starts `CLOSING` and takes the first of them as its `firstItemId` (the log line `automation session state changed` names it); its `belowFilterCount` stays on the part that keeps the id. So the sessions are the same runs of buys whatever order discovery finds them in. A session's id is the SHA-256 of `wallet|` the item it opened for and never changes; `firstItemId` is that item while it stands, or, once a replacement removed it, the session's earliest remaining item, so it is not always the earliest buy and need not hash to the id. Every `tick-interval` (1 min), a session starts `CLOSING` only once a complete discovery scan covers more than `session-idle` after its last buy (the watermark row's `updated_at` less the 60 s margin), so no buy still undiscovered can join it; the clock alone never closes a session. After changing `trenching.automation.wallet`, the old wallet is no longer scanned, so its open sessions never close on their own: close them by hand with `POST /api/automation/sessions/{id}/close`. It closes (`IDLE`) once its items are settled. In phase 0 a `DISCOVERED` item counts as settled, so sessions close on their own; in phase 1 only terminal items (`SIMULATED`, `FAILED`, `ABANDONED`, `SKIPPED_NOT_FIRST_BUY`) do. `POST /api/automation/sessions/{id}/close` ends one by hand (`MANUAL`) and abandons its pending items. Once that has committed, every capture operation an abandoned item had started and that has not ended is cancelled through the capture console's `cancel` control (request id `auto-cancel-<step>-<first 24 hex of the item id>-<attempt>`), so it never runs on, or retries, outside the request cap; one already ended is left alone.
- **Capture (phase 1).** Every `tick-interval` the pipeline drives each due item of an open or closing session, oldest buy first, through the console's own capture services, as a user would: `CREATE_SESSION` (a wallet + token session for the buy's mint in `FULL` cutoff mode, with the form's wallet-token defaults), `WALLET`, `MARKET`, `RATES`, `EXPORT`, `QUALIFY` and `REGISTER`, after which the item is `CAPTURED` with its `datasetId`. The export queues its own qualification, which the item follows; a later qualification attempt runs under the automation's request id. Every call uses the request id `auto-<step>-<first 24 hex of the item id>-<attempt>`, so a restart gets the same capture session or operation back instead of a second one. Items left `DISCOVERED` in sessions closed during phase 0 are never captured; they stay `DISCOVERED`.
- **Waits and retries.** At most one capture operation starts per tick, and only while the capture worker is idle and no operation automation started is still running or waiting for its own retry; a worker busy with manual use just makes the item wait for the next tick. `MARKET` starts no earlier than the buy's block time plus `market-delay` (35 min); until then the item is `WAITING`. A `PAUSED` operation waits until it is resumed or cancelled in the console. A step whose operation ends `FAILED`, `INCOMPLETE`, `INTERRUPTED` or `CANCELLED`, or whose call the capture console refuses, runs again under the next attempt, up to `max-step-attempts` (2); then the item is `FAILED` with the operation's code and its message, cut to 512 characters. Three refusals are not failed attempts: `CAPTURE_BUSY` waits for the next tick; `NOT_CONFIGURED` (no `SOLANA_RPC_URL`, or reference rates switched off) leaves the item waiting at its step with no attempt charged, logged once per tick at `WARN`, until the backend is configured; and `CAPTURE_NOT_FOUND`, a capture session or operation deleted from the console, fails the item at once with that code. An operation started for an item that a manual close abandoned in the meantime is cancelled at once. A verdict whose first buy is unproven (`NO_TARGET_BUY`, `FIRST_BUY_UNPROVEN`, `FIRST_BUY_OUTSIDE_TOKEN_ACCOUNT`) ends the item `SKIPPED_NOT_FIRST_BUY`. Wallet and market operations send `operation-budget` and the `recovery` limits. Each item records `requestsUsed`, its capture session's provider requests, whenever a step ends. An unexpected failure is logged at `ERROR` with the item id and the tick goes on; every state change logs `automation item advanced itemId=… step=… state=…` at `INFO`.
- **Sweep (phase 1).** Every `tick-interval` up to 2 `CAPTURED` items whose sweep is due, oldest buy first, replay every entry of the active sweep plan (`sweep-plan`, `decu-sweep-1`: V00 is `SimulationConfig.agreed()`, V01–V09 each change one factor) in label order, each as a dataset-referenced run (`SimulationRuns.start(datasetId, config, DATASET_REFERENCE)`), so no dataset copy is stored. Each entry gets one `automation_sweep_runs` row, keyed by the SHA-256 of `item|plan|label`, with its `runId`, `configurationHash` and `engineVersion`, its `outcome`, `positionState`, `isSignal`, `realizedPnl` and `worstCasePnl`. The outcome is the item's cycle in the run: the position's close reason, the reason it stayed open (`HORIZON_REACHED`, `UNRESOLVED`, `MIGRATED`), or the reason it bought nothing (`NO_DIP`, `SIGNAL_BELOW_MIN_SIZE`, …); `NO_CYCLE` when the run holds none (among several cycles, the one of the item's own buy signature). An entry the replay refuses (`MARKET_COVERAGE_ENDED`, `SELL_CHECK_NEEDS_TRADES`, `SIGNAL_SIZE_NEEDS_TRADES`, and `DATASET_NOT_FOUND` or `DATASET_INVALID` for a registered dataset that is gone or unreadable) is stored with that code as its outcome and no run, and is never retried. P&L is SOL at scale 12: `realizedPnl` is the closed position's, else 0; `worstCasePnl` is the same, except that a position still open counts at minus its entry's whole cost (notional, entry fee and transaction cost). Once every entry has its row the item is `SIMULATED`. Runs are idempotent, so a repeat gets the same run ids, and an entry with a row is never run again. An unexpected failure stores nothing for that entry and is logged at `ERROR` with the item id and label; the item's sweep then counts as a failed attempt (its `step` becomes `SWEEP`, `stepAttempt` the next attempt, `failureCode` `SWEEP_FAILED`) and waits 5 minutes, doubled for each further failure, in `nextActionAt`; after `max-step-attempts` the item is `FAILED` with `SWEEP_FAILED`. Only items whose sweep is due are picked, so a failing item never holds a tick's slots. Rows are inserted, never merged over a stored row, so a concurrent duplicate fails on the unique key and the row already stored stands. Each run opens at `/api/runs/{id}`. Changing `sweep-plan` starts fresh: items already `SIMULATED` are not swept again under the new plan.
- **Scoring (phase 1).** Every `tick-interval`, each `CLOSED` session with no evaluation under the active plan is scored, oldest first buy first, once two things hold: its items have sweep rows under the active plan, and every earlier session of its wallet (by first buy) is `CLOSED`, so its window is complete. A phase-0 session, a session whose items never reached the sweep, and a session swept under another plan are therefore never scored under the active plan: switching `sweep-plan` scores only the sessions swept under the new plan, with windows that start afresh, instead of adding `NO_RESULT` rows to past sessions. A session closed by hand is scored at once, after its pending items, captured ones included, are abandoned, unless an earlier session is still open; a later tick then scores it. The window is the session and up to `evaluation-window-sessions − 1` (6) earlier closed sessions of the wallet already scored under the plan, by first buy time; its ids are stored in order. For each entry: `signals` counts the simulated items whose run is a signal (every outcome except `SIGNAL_BELOW_MIN_SIZE`, `SIGNAL_SIZE_UNAVAILABLE`, `REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE`, `NO_CYCLE` and the refusals; a later skip such as `NO_DIP` is a signal with 0 P&L); `unscored` counts the `FAILED` and `ABANDONED` items, the simulated items without a row for the entry, and the items whose entry was refused or whose run has no usable result (`REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE`, `SIGNAL_SIZE_UNAVAILABLE`, `NO_CYCLE`); `scored` counts every other item with a run for the entry, so the two never overlap. A buy below the minimum size is a valid non-signal, and `SKIPPED_NOT_FIRST_BUY` items count nowhere. `realizedPnl` and `worstCasePnl` sum the signals. The status is `SUCCESS` with at least `min-signals` (10) signals and a worst-case P&L strictly above `min-net-pnl-sol` (0), `NO_SUCCESS` with enough signals otherwise, and `NO_RESULT` with fewer. All entries of a session are inserted in one transaction, one row per (session, plan, label); a session scored before keeps its rows, and when a manual close and the tick score the same session at once, the second hits the unique key and returns the first one's rows. Statuses rank `SUCCESS` › `NO_SUCCESS` › `NO_RESULT`; a session's status is its best entry's, by rank then worst-case P&L.
- **Request cap.** Once capture is on, `max-requests-per-24h` (5,000) bounds the provider requests of the rolling 24 hours, manual captures included; `/status` shows the day's use against it. Before a `WALLET` or `MARKET` operation starts, the day's requests plus `recovery.requests` (1,200), the most that operation may spend over all its automatic retries, must stay within the cap; otherwise the item is `BLOCKED_BUDGET` and is checked again on every tick, so an operation automation starts never crosses the cap. The default is a guess: run phase 0 for 2 to 3 nights, compare the buys per night with the Helius allowance, then set the cap at about 70% of it. Discovery itself sends at most 22 requests per page, and transactions already in the shared cache are free.
- **Enable it locally** by setting `trenching.automation.enabled=true` and `trenching.automation.wallet` (a public base58 address) for the backend process, for example as the `TRENCHING_AUTOMATION_ENABLED` and `TRENCHING_AUTOMATION_WALLET` environment variables, with `SOLANA_RPC_URL` configured, and keep the PC awake overnight. Startup fails, naming the property, on a value out of bounds, on a missing wallet while enabled, or when `capture-min-signal-sol` is above the smallest minimum signal size of the sweep plan (`sweep-plan`, `decu-sweep-1`, whose smallest is 1.5 SOL). A single backend instance is assumed.

**API usage.** Every HTTP request the backend sends to the Solana RPC or Binance is counted where it is sent, retries and connection probes included, and reported by `GET /api/usage` for the console's API usage page. The units are fixed and named in every response: `requests` are HTTP requests sent, `failedRequests` are transport errors, non-2xx replies other than 429 and JSON-RPC errors, `rateLimitedResponses` are HTTP 429 replies, `responseBytes` are response body bytes received, and `cacheHits` are `getTransaction` answers served by the shared transaction cache without a request. They are request counts, not provider billing credits. `period` is `LAST_HOUR` (default), `LAST_24_HOURS`, `LAST_7_DAYS` or `ALL`; each report states its exact `from` (aligned to 5 minutes) and `to` (now). `captureId` narrows the report to one capture. Counts are kept in memory and added to 5-minute UTC buckets in the `api_usage_buckets` table every `trenching.api-usage.flush-interval` (15 s); reports include counts not yet written. A clean shutdown writes them, but a hard kill loses up to one interval (15 s) of counts. Gradle CLI captures (`captureWalletHistory`, `captureReplayMarkets`, `captureReferenceRates`) do not start the database and are not counted. Rows are never pruned.

```powershell
.\gradlew.bat build -PwithConsole
java -jar build/libs/trenching-bot-0.0.1-SNAPSHOT.jar --spring.profiles.active=local
```

On Bash, set `JAVA_HOME` to your JDK 21 before `./gradlew build -PwithConsole`. The opt-in build runs `npm ci` and `npm run build`, then includes `frontend/dist` under `static/console/`. Plain `./gradlew build` remains Node-free and removes bundled console resources left by a previous opt-in build.

## Real wallet history capture

The approved next increment uses Helius standard HTTP RPC for a user-run, bounded mainnet sample. `captureWalletHistory` freezes up to 100 distinct successful address-reference signatures from a bounded newest-first scan (`-PscanPages`, default 20 pages of 1,000), counts the failed references it skips without fetching them, retains unavailable/non-signer outcomes and saves audited transaction evidence under ignored build output. `-Pscope=pumpfun-user-buys` scans the wallet's derived Pump.fun user volume accumulator instead of the wallet, which skips other accounts' bot transactions and finds the wallet's own Pump.fun buys ([decision](docs/adr/20261006-pumpfun-user-accumulator-discovery.md)). `inspectWalletHistory` verifies that evidence offline without RPC credentials or PostgreSQL. Neither task runs as part of build/test or starts the backend. Set `SOLANA_RPC_URL` in the capture terminal's environment; the command does not load `.env` files, and an IDE run configuration does not configure a separate terminal.

Follow [real capture instructions](docs/REAL_DATA_CAPTURE.md) for the selected wallet, scan bound, stop reasons, request/byte budgets, resuming a frozen sample and safe inspection. Capture directories from before successful-reference discovery are refused; capture into a new directory. `inspectWalletHistory` also decodes a complete capture offline: only a SOL-quoted Pump.fun bonding-curve buy (`buy` or `buy_exact_quote_in_v3`) signed by the wallet becomes a wallet transaction. Every other selected transaction keeps an explicit decode outcome, and inspection counts rejection reasons and the Pump.fun instructions found (see the [decoder decision](docs/adr/20261003-pumpfun-bonding-curve-buy-decoder.md) and its [V3 amendment](docs/adr/20261010-pumpfun-exact-quote-in-v3-buys.md)). Capture runs stop at a per-run deadline, 10 minutes unless `-PoperationTimeoutMinutes=<1..30>` is given, and print `stopReason`; re-run the same command to resume. A wallet capture alone is not a simulation dataset: it needs the market capture below for same-pair prices at the relevant slots, and `exportRecordedReplay` combines the two. Actual wallet P&L and complete current/closed token-account history are outside this sample's scope. See the [approved plan](docs/plans/archive/20261003-real-wallet-replay.md), the [capture decision](docs/adr/20261003-bounded-wallet-history-capture.md) and the [successful-reference discovery decision](docs/adr/20261003-successful-reference-discovery.md).

`captureReplayMarkets` is the second user-run stage. It requires a complete, decodable wallet capture (`-PwalletCaptureDirectory`, no default) and checks it before it reads `SOLANA_RPC_URL`. For the wallet's first buy of each mint, at slot S, it captures the mint's successful transactions in [S-50, S+4500]: about 10 s before the buy, the buy's own slot, and the dip-entry horizon (about 30 minutes) after it ([decision](docs/adr/20261022-market-window-lead.md); captures from before market manifest version 4 keep [S+1, S+4500]); a busy mint can hold thousands of trades there. Discovery lists the mint's signatures newest-first, through the source buy down to the window's start, bounded by `-PscanPages` (default 20) per window, and fetches at most `-PmaxTransactions` (default 300, up to 50,000) details in total, with request and byte budgets of up to 60,000 and 4 GiB. A window that hits either bound is reported as `COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE` or `OVER_CAP` and yields no observations. `-PcutoffMode=DECIDED` stops each window once its outcome under the agreed strategy (`SimulationConfig.agreed()`, its 30 s dip window and 3/4-minute time exit) is decided and the recording reaches at least 4 minutes after the buy's block time, so a token is typically recorded for about 4–5 minutes ([decided and full mode](docs/REAL_DATA_CAPTURE.md#decided-and-full-mode)); the default is `FULL`. A skip such as `NO_DIP` is decided at the first trade after the 30 s window but still recorded through 4 minutes, and a cutoff recorded under `dip-paper-v3` reopens on resume. While a decision waits on time, the cutoff reads the prefix end's block time at most once per 100-transaction batch. When a window's coverage completes, the capture reads the block time of its last recorded slot once (`getBlockTime`, stepping back over a skipped slot; counted as API usage purpose `BLOCK_TIME`) and stores it in the window's market manifest (version 5) as `throughBlockTime`; a failed read leaves it null and is retried on resume. A rerun with a larger `-PscanPages` (up to 500) extends a `COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE` window from its saved cursor. Each completely discovered window has its details checkpointed before discovery starts for the next window. The console can validate that case alone, prepare its exact rate-minute set and export/import its own canonical v6 dataset; corrupt or incomplete siblings remain visible without blocking it. `inspectReplayMarkets` still re-verifies the whole capture offline and extracts one `LAST_TRADE` observation per Pump.fun bonding-curve trade event, now (extractor `PUMPFUN_BONDING_CURVE_TRADE_V2`) also carrying the event's trader, side and amounts, with every exclusion counted by reason ([observation decision](docs/adr/20261004-pumpfun-last-trade-observations.md)). `captureReferenceRates` and `exportRecordedReplay` also keep their full-capture requirements. The rate command records the Binance SOL/USDT minute rate of every captured trade for the market-cap rule ([decision](docs/adr/20261008-reference-rates-in-dataset.md)); the export command turns all three verified captures into a canonical v6 dataset offline, recording how far each window was observed and, from manifest version 5, the block time it was observed through, which proves a time exit on a token that stopped trading (v3 to v5 datasets still load and replay unchanged; a re-export has a new dataset id, see [the format](docs/REPLAY_FORMAT.md) and the [decision](docs/adr/20261015-chart-trade-actors-and-mcap.md)) and refuses a mismatched or incomplete market capture. See [market capture](docs/REAL_DATA_CAPTURE.md#market-capture), [independent case validation](docs/REAL_DATA_CAPTURE.md#independent-case-validation-in-the-console) and [export](docs/REAL_DATA_CAPTURE.md#export-to-a-replay-dataset). `calibrateExecution` verifies the curve math against each recorded buy, measures latency error and reports network fees and other lamport costs entirely offline; see [calibration](docs/REAL_DATA_CAPTURE.md#calibrate-execution-offline).
