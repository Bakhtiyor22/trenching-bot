# Automated capture and sweep evaluation

Accepted 2026-11-02 with the approved [automated capture and sweep plan](../plans/archive/20261102-decu-capture-sweep-automation.md) (Key Decisions 1–5 and 8, Domain Rules 5–26), including the user's decision of 2026-11-03 on `REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE` (Rule 22). Amended 2026-11-03 by the review fixes: the plan's clarifications of Rule 24 (disjoint `scored`/`unscored`, earlier sessions closed first), the reviewer decisions on closing after a complete scan and on plan switches, catch-up session placement, sweep retries and capture cancellation (§2, §3, §4, §6, §7 and Consequences); and again on 2026-11-03 by the second review's fixes, approved by the user: sessions independent of discovery order, bulk merges and their tie-break, the indexing margin, catch-up completion, buys without a block time, capture waiting for a catch-up, morning closing, the wallet switch and the lookback bound (§2, §3, §4 and Consequences). It builds on the [capture console decision](20261010-capture-console.md), the [wallet + token capture](20261010-wallet-token-capture.md), the [fee-inclusive signal size](20261102-fee-inclusive-signal-size.md) and [dataset-referenced runs](20261102-dataset-referenced-runs.md).

## Context

Testing the agreed strategy on Decu's buys is manual today: pick a buy from the wallet activity list, create a wallet + token capture, start its wallet, market, rates and export operations, register the dataset, start runs one configuration at a time and compare them by eye. Decu trades about 19:00–03:00 UTC+5, the number of buys per night is unknown, and the provider is the Helius free tier. With about one trading cycle per token, one token says little about a configuration; the spec (Section 12) judges a strategy on net P&L across every signal, skips included.

The user asked for an unattended loop on this PC (movable to a server later): discover each buy, capture it once its market window has passed, replay a fixed set of variations on it, and score every variation over rolling sessions, with results in the console and nothing adopted automatically.

## Decision

1. **Automation is a sub-package of the console.** `console.automation` drives the capture console's services (`CaptureSessionService`, `CaptureOperationService`, `CaptureCatalogService`) directly, so their internals do not become a public port, and loads only where they exist: the local profile with the console enabled, and `trenching.automation.enabled=true` for the scheduler.
2. **Discovery is a polled watermark, not a batch.** Inside a polling window (18:00–05:00 UTC+5 by default), every 10 minutes it pages the wallet's activity newest first back to a per-wallet watermark (slot, signature, instruction, inner index). The watermark advances only after a complete scan, in the same transaction that stores the items; a scan cut short by its page limit keeps it, and the unique keys absorb repeats. A wallet's first scan reaches back a fixed lookback (12 h). A scan cut short stores a resume cursor; a first scan cut short before any Pump.fun action stores it too, under a placeholder watermark before every action (slot `-1`), so no backlog is ever restarted from the top. A first scan also stops at the first page whose oldest listed transaction is older than its lookback, by the block time the signature listing already carries, so a history with no decodable trade costs no extra pages. The watermark row's `updated_at` records the start of the last complete scan: one from the newest transaction to its bound, or the one that began a catch-up a later scan completed (kept meanwhile in `catch_up_started_at`), so steady activity that cuts every scan from the newest transaction short cannot freeze it. A scan covers every buy up to its start less a 60 s indexing margin. Outside the polling window discovery scans only while an open session's idle time has passed without a covering scan, about 21 requests per tick, so the night's last session closes the same morning. Ids are SHA-256 hashes of their input, so a restart converges on the same rows.
3. **One item per (wallet, mint), filtered before capture.** Only a mint's first discovered buy becomes an item (spec P2: only the first buy triggers), and only when its curve payment raised by a 300 bps fee margin is above 1.5 SOL. The filter must not exceed the smallest minimum signal size of the active sweep plan, so it never drops a buy some configuration would count; startup refuses a filter above it, or a plan entry with no minimum. Whether the buy really is the wallet's first is proven by the capture, not by discovery. A catch-up can find a mint's earlier buy after its later one; the earlier buy then takes the item while no capture step has touched it (`DISCOVERED` with no step, or `FAILED` for a missing block time) and its session is open, and otherwise the item is kept and the earlier buy logged, since the capture's first-buy proof covers it. No item starts its capture while its wallet has a catch-up in progress, so the real first buy is found first.
4. **Sessions end on idleness, as discovery sees it.** A session is a run of buys with no gap longer than 60 minutes. A buy joins the open session it fits by time, from 60 minutes before its first buy to 60 minutes after its last, so a catch-up that reads older buys extends a session backwards or opens an earlier one; a buy that fits two open sessions merges them into the one discovered first (then earlier first buy, then smaller id), moving the other's items and sweep rows in bulk. A replaced item's session is rebuilt from the items left, split where a gap longer than 60 minutes opens and deleted when empty, so the sessions are the same runs of buys in whatever order discovery finds them. A session's id is the SHA-256 of `wallet|` the item it opened for and never changes; `firstItemId` is that item while it stands, else the session's earliest remaining item. A buy without a block time joins the latest open session without changing its span, or a session of its own that starts `CLOSING`, so it never places or bridges a session by discovery time. A session starts closing only once a complete discovery scan covers more than 60 minutes after its last buy (the watermark's `updated_at` less the indexing margin; reviewer decision, 2026-11-03), because a buy not yet discovered within that time would otherwise open a new session; the clock alone never closes one. It closes once all its items are settled, or by hand, which abandons the rest and cancels, after the close has committed, the capture operations they had running, so none of them spends requests outside the cap. Phase 0 (`capture-enabled=false`) only discovers, so its sessions close with their items still `DISCOVERED`; it measures buys per night and the filter's yield before any capture spends requests.
5. **Capture is `FULL`.** Some variations hold longer than the agreed 4 minutes, which a `DECIDED` window would refuse as `MARKET_COVERAGE_ENDED`. Market capture starts at block time + 35 minutes, after the 4,500-slot window has passed. A rolling 24-hour request cap, manual captures included, bounds the extra cost.
6. **Every configuration runs on every buy.** The sweep plan (`decu-sweep-1`: the agreed configuration and nine variations of one factor each) is fixed in code; a changed list is a new plan id. All entries run in order, offline, as dataset-referenced runs; stopping at the first success would let the order of the entries decide the result. A tick sweeps at most two captured items, oldest buy first, and stores one row per (item, plan, label), keyed by the SHA-256 of `item|plan|label`, with the run's id, configuration hash and engine version. The row's outcome is read from the run's own stored cycle of the item's buy, through the same store ports the console's run detail reads: the close reason, the reason a position stayed open, the reason no order filled, or `NO_CYCLE` for a run without one. An entry the replay refuses (`MARKET_COVERAGE_ENDED`, `SELL_CHECK_NEEDS_TRADES`, `SIGNAL_SIZE_NEEDS_TRADES`, and `DATASET_NOT_FOUND` or `DATASET_INVALID`) is stored with that code and no run, and never retried. An unexpected failure stores nothing for its entry and counts as a failed sweep attempt on the item (`step` `SWEEP`, `step_attempt`, `next_action_at` 5 minutes later, doubling): only due items are swept, so a failing item cannot hold a tick's slots, and after `max-step-attempts` it is `FAILED` with `SWEEP_FAILED`. An entry with a row is never run again, and rows are real inserts, so a concurrent duplicate fails on the key. Once every entry has a row the item is `SIMULATED`. No transaction is held across a run.
7. **Success is judged across sessions.** An evaluation per closing session and entry covers that session and the 6 before it. An item is a signal for an entry when its first buy is proven and the entry's run did not end `SIGNAL_BELOW_MIN_SIZE` or `SIGNAL_SIZE_UNAVAILABLE`; later skips are signals with 0 P&L. A run that ended `REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE` is checked before the size, so its size is unknown: it is unscored, like a failed capture, not a signal (user decision, 2026-11-03). `NO_CYCLE` and a refused entry are not signals either. An open position counts at minus its entry cost (notional, entry fee and transaction cost). `unscored` counts the failed and abandoned items, the simulated items without a row for the entry, and the items whose entry was refused or whose run has no usable result (`SIGNAL_SIZE_UNAVAILABLE`, `REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE`, `NO_CYCLE`); `scored` counts every other item with a run for the entry, so the two never overlap (plan clarification, 2026-11-03). `SUCCESS` needs at least 10 signals and a worst-case net P&L above 0 SOL; otherwise `NO_SUCCESS`, or `NO_RESULT` with fewer signals. A session's status is its best entry's, by status rank, then worst-case P&L. Results are flagged only; nothing is promoted to the default.
   - A session is scored under the active plan once it is `CLOSED`, its items have sweep rows under that plan, and every earlier session of its wallet is `CLOSED`, so its window is complete (plan clarification, 2026-11-03). A phase-0 session, one whose items never reached the sweep, and one swept under another plan are never scored under it. Pending sessions are scored oldest first buy first, so each window finds the earlier sessions already scored. The window takes only earlier sessions that have evaluations under the same plan. A manual close abandons the session's pending items, captured ones included, and scores it at once, or leaves it to a later tick while an earlier session is still open.
   - All entries of a session are inserted in one transaction, unique on (session, plan, label); a repeat returns the stored rows and replaces nothing. When a manual close and the tick score a session at once, the second to commit hits the key and returns the first one's rows.
   - Changing `sweep-plan` starts fresh (reviewer decision, 2026-11-03): items already `SIMULATED` are not swept again, so only sessions swept under the new plan are scored under it, with windows of their own; past sessions get no `NO_RESULT` rows.
8. **PostgreSQL holds the automation state; no Kafka or ClickHouse.** Five tables (`automation_watermarks`, `automation_sessions`, `automation_items`, `automation_sweep_runs`, `automation_evaluations`) form a ledger keyed by deterministic ids, with unique keys for every idempotency rule. The scheduler is an in-process `@Scheduled` trigger with fixed delays, so ticks never overlap; a single instance is assumed. Captures keep their files.

## Alternatives

- **A top-level `automation` module.** Rejected: it would need a public capture contract out of `console` for a single consumer.
- **A fixed nightly batch.** Rejected: a buy can be captured only after its window has passed, and polling is cheap because repeat transactions come from the shared cache.
- **`DECIDED` capture.** Rejected: it cannot serve variations that hold longer than the agreed clocks.
- **Stopping the sweep at the first success, or scoring per token.** Rejected: the first makes the entry order decide; the second is noise with about one cycle per token.
- **Kafka and ClickHouse.** Rejected for now: one producer, one consumer and a few thousand rows a night fit PostgreSQL and an in-process scheduler; a broker and a second store would add operations without a consumer that needs them.

## Consequences

- Automation needs the PC awake through the polling window; `/status` shows the last tick, so a missed night is visible.
- The five tables exist in every profile, so production applies their reference SQL ([V20261102120000](../schema/V20261102120000__automation.sql)) before deploying, though automation never runs there.
- A buy discovered without a block time cannot be scheduled and is stored `FAILED`; a buy below the filter is counted on the session it falls in, never captured.
- A backlog larger than the discovery page limit is worked through one capped scan per tick from its resume cursor until a scan completes.
- A session closes only after a complete scan has covered its idle time. One whose last buy is before the window's end and whose idle time ends after it is scanned for in the morning and closes then; a session a daytime buy opens or extends (including a pre-window one a morning scan extends past the window's end) waits for the window to open, so a wallet that keeps buying cannot keep the morning scans going; a long catch-up delays closing until it completes; the last complete scan is stored, so a restart changes nothing.
- A catch-up can merge two open sessions, extend one backwards or, by replacing an item, shrink or split one, so a session's first buy can move and its `firstItemId` is not always the earliest buy. The partition into sessions does not depend on discovery order; the ids, and which part keeps a split session's `belowFilterCount`, do. A buy without a block time is attached to whichever session is latest when it is found; it is replaced by an earlier buy of its mint like any item no capture step touched, also in a `CLOSING` session, which is deleted if that leaves it empty, and a session left with only such buys starts `CLOSING` with the first of them as its `firstItemId`.
- After the configured wallet changes, the old wallet is no longer scanned, so its open sessions never close on their own; they are closed by hand (`POST /api/automation/sessions/{id}/close`).
- A buy found during a catch-up waits for it to complete before its capture starts, so capture can start up to a catch-up's length later.
- Two backend instances would race to start capture operations: the unique keys and `requestId` idempotency keep the state consistent, but provider requests could be spent twice.
- A manual close cancels the abandoned items' running or retry-waiting operations after it commits; a cancel the capture console refuses or that fails is logged, not retried, and an operation that already ended is left as it is.
- An unconfigured RPC (or disabled reference rates) holds items at their step without charging an attempt; a capture deleted from the console fails its item with `CAPTURE_NOT_FOUND`.
- Evaluations are only as wide as the captured sessions: failed and unscored items lower the signal count rather than blocking an evaluation, and `unscored` is shown on every row. A session none of whose items reached the sweep is never evaluated, so its failed items appear in no window.
- `scored` and `unscored` never overlap: together they count every failed, abandoned and simulated item of the window (`SKIPPED_NOT_FIRST_BUY` items count in neither).
- Under the agreed `POST_TRADE` measure the reference is checked before the size and reads the same observation, so on a v4+ dataset a missing buy observation ends `REFERENCE_UNAVAILABLE`; `SIGNAL_SIZE_UNAVAILABLE` needs a buy observation without its trade, which a schema v4+ dataset refuses, so in practice it does not occur. Both are unscored.
- A session waits for its evaluation until every earlier session of its wallet is closed, so a window is never scored without an earlier session; one earlier session stuck in capture holds back the scoring of every later one.
- Switching the sweep plan starts the scoreboard afresh for that plan; the earlier plan's evaluations stay as they are.
- The console reads the scoreboard (`GET /api/automation/evaluations`) and sweep runs (`GET /api/automation/sweep-runs`) with server-side sort and filter, and filters sessions by capture health (`health=ISSUES|OK`: a failed or abandoned item, or a refused sweep entry). The status rank is repeated as a SQL `CASE` in the ranking queries, kept in step with `EvaluationStatus.rank` by the controller tests.

## Amendment 2026-11-03: always-on discovery, trading days and day scoring

Decided with the user on 2026-11-03 ([plan](../plans/archive/20261103-always-on-automation-trading-days-backfill.md), Key Decisions 1 to 5; the backfill, Key Decisions 6 to 10, follows in its own amendment). They change Decisions 2 (the polling window), 4 (sessions), 7 (scoring by session) and 8 (the stored evaluations table) above; nothing else in this record changes, and no simulation semantics, engine or sweep plan is touched.

1. **Always-on is a mode, not a 24-hour window.** `trenching.automation.poll-always` (default `false`). When true, scheduled discovery scans at every discovery interval and neither the polling window nor the morning scans (`closeAwaitsScan`) are consulted; when false, behaviour is exactly as before. A window of 05:00 to 05:00 stays refused, since a mode states the intent explicitly in configuration and in `/status` (`mode`).
2. **"Run now" queues one discovery pass on the scheduling thread.** `POST /api/automation/discovery/run` returns `202` and hands one `discover` to the application's `TaskScheduler`, whose single thread also runs the timed discovery, the pipeline, the sweep and the session ticks; a second request while a pass is queued or running adds nothing. The pass ignores the window in both modes. Running the scan on the HTTP thread was rejected: it would race the scheduled scan over the watermark row and fail on its `@Version`. With automation off the endpoint answers `409 AUTOMATION_DISABLED`.
3. **A trading day runs from 12:00 to 12:00 UTC+5 and is named by its starting date** (`trading-day-start`, in `zone`). Noon is the quietest point of the wallet's day, so a night is never split. A session never crosses a day boundary: a buy joins an open session only on the same trading day, and merges and regrouping respect it too. No column stores the day; a day's sessions are those whose `first_buy_at` is in its range. The wallet's watermark row records `covered_from`, the start of its first scan's lookback, so a day can be judged covered; a backfill will move it back.
4. **Scoring is per trading day and computed on read, not stored.** Day D's row for each sweep entry tallies the items of the days D − 6 to D (`evaluation-window-sessions`, kept under its name, now counts calendar trading days, empty days included) under the unchanged Rules 22 to 25. A backfill fills older days after newer ones were scored, so stored verdicts would go stale and Decision 7's "nothing is replaced" rule would forbid fixing them; a read over a few hundred rows is cheap. The `automation_evaluations` entity and its writing are removed; the table stays in the database.
5. **A day has a verdict only when its whole window is complete.** A day is complete when discovery has covered all of it, every session in it is `CLOSED` and every item is terminal; otherwise the row is `PENDING`, naming the oldest incomplete day. `PENDING` is a state, not a fourth verdict. In phase 0 no item is ever terminal, so no day completes.

Consequences: sessions keep their idle-gap meaning underneath the days. The per-session `Scoring` of Decision 7 (a session's window of 7 sessions, its stored rows, the manual close scoring a session) no longer exists; the console reads `/days`, `/days/{date}` and a changed `/evaluations` (keyed by trading day), and the session endpoints lose their evaluation fields. Rows of past days change on the next read after anything earlier fills in.

## Amendment 2026-11-03 (2): backfill

Decided with the user on 2026-11-03 ([plan](../plans/archive/20261103-always-on-automation-trading-days-backfill.md), Key Decisions 6 to 10). It adds a date-range job on top of the previous amendment; no simulation semantics, engine or sweep plan is touched.

6. **A backfill extends history backwards; it never overlaps what is already covered.** Live discovery covers `[covered_from, discoveredThrough]` without gaps. A capturing backfill must end at or before `covered_from` (its end is clipped to it) and overlap no other capturing backfill; when it finishes and its end meets `covered_from`, that moves back to its start. This keeps two discoveries from writing items for the same time. A dry run writes no item, so it may cover any range. Finished capturing backfills (`DONE`, or `STOPPED_BUDGET` whose scan reached the range start) join the coverage ranges that decide whether a day is covered.
7. **Backfill items go through the same pipeline, after live items.** Buys become ordinary `automation_items` with `backfill_id`, in sessions tagged with it, so capture, the sweep and day scoring are unchanged. The pipeline takes due live items first and a backfill's after them, each oldest buy first, with the single capture worker. A backfill's items are not captured until its scan is over, because a later tick can still find an earlier buy of a mint that replaces the item; the live idle rule and live placement ignore a backfill's sessions for the same reason.
8. **A backfill has its own request budget inside the shared 24-hour cap.** Spend is its discovery requests plus its items' `requests_used`. A page of discovery (at most 22 requests) or a wallet or market operation (one recovery allowance) starts only if the spend plus that fits the budget; an item the budget cannot cover ends `FAILED` with `BACKFILL_BUDGET`, unscored, and the backfill ends `STOPPED_BUDGET`. Backfill discovery also pauses while the rolling total is within `backfill-reserve-requests` of the cap.
9. **The filters narrow a backfill; they never widen what the sweep can use.** `minSignalSol` may not go below `capture-min-signal-sol` (every sweep entry needs at least 1.5 SOL, so smaller buys would only spend requests); `mints` limits the items to up to 50 tokens; a dry run writes nothing and reports buys per day, distinct mints, discovery requests and an estimate (median `requests_used` of captured items, rounded up, times the buys passing the filter, or none while nothing has been captured).
10. **Ranges are given in trading days.** `fromDay` and `toDay` (inclusive) become `[fromDay 12:00, toDay + 1 12:00)` in the trading zone.

Implementation choices the plan left open. The wallet listing can only be entered after a signature, never at a time, so a backfill's first scan tick enters after the wallet's earliest stored buy at or past the range end and drops what lies between by block time; with no such buy it starts at the newest transaction. This needs no change to the wallet activity contract and spends no page on the live days beyond that buy. A budget-stopped scan keeps its cursor, which is how a stopped scan is told from a finished one. Actions without a block time cannot be placed in a range and are left out of a backfill. A backfill's earlier buy of a mint replaces a live item of the mint whatever state its session is in, as long as no capture step has touched it; only the backfill's own open sessions can be regrouped, so a live session left empty is removed and one that keeps other items keeps its span.

Consequences: `automation_backfills` and the `backfill_id` columns of items and sessions are additive; production applies them by hand (`docs/schema/V20261103120000__automation_backfill.sql`). `POST/GET /api/automation/backfills`, `GET /api/automation/backfills/{id}` and `POST /api/automation/backfills/{id}/cancel` are new, `/status` gains `activeBackfillId` and the session and item responses gain `backfillId`.

## Amendment 2026-11-03 (3): review fixes

Decided with the user on 2026-11-03 after the review of the first two amendments' implementation (decisions D1 to D5). They amend Decisions 6 to 10 above and the plan's Rules 19, 32, 33, 35, 37, 40 and 42; no simulation semantics, engine or sweep plan is touched.

- **D1: the reserve covers a backfill's captures.** A backfill's wallet or market step starts only if the rolling 24-hour total plus one operation's recovery allowance stays within `max-requests-per-24h` less `backfill-reserve-requests`; a live step still needs only the cap. Backfill discovery already paused at the cap less the reserve. Startup refuses a reserve below `recovery.requests` plus one discovery tick (`max-discovery-pages` pages of 22 requests) or at the cap, and the default rises from 1,000 to 1,400 (1,200 + 5 x 22 = 1,310). A capturing backfill's budget must be at least `recovery.requests` plus one page (1,222), or it could never start an operation, and startup refuses a `backfill-max-requests` below it; a dry run keeps the minimum of 100.
- **D2: the phase 0 backlog is captured once capture is on.** `DISCOVERED` items of sessions closed while capture was off are due like any other item, whatever their session's state, and go through capture and the sweep, so their days complete with real data. Priority, each class oldest buy first: live items of sessions still `OPEN` or `CLOSING`, then this backlog (live items of `CLOSED` sessions), then a backfill's items. With capture on a session cannot close while an item of it is non-terminal, so the `CLOSED` class holds exactly the backlog and no buy of the current trading day waits behind it. A closed session never reopens; the day's completeness reads the items, not the session's state.
- **D3: a stopped or cancelled backfill can be run again.** A scan records `scanned_to`, the second after the oldest transaction listed by the last page it read (a transaction of the same second may be unread), and the range start once it finished. An ended capturing backfill (done, stopped by its budget or cancelled) covers `[scanned_to, range end)`, and `covered_from` moves back to `scanned_to` when its range end meets it, when it ends (amendment 4 withdraws this move for a stopped, cancelled or filtered backfill). Only an active or `DONE` capturing backfill blocks overlap, so a new one over the same dates is not blocked (amendment 4: it is clipped to the live floor only and re-reads the range), and no range is counted twice (coverage ranges merge). A buy a later backfill finds again takes over the stored item of another backfill that produced nothing: one no capture step touched, one `FAILED` with `BACKFILL_BUDGET`, or one abandoned before a dataset; anything that captured stays. Cancelling a `STOPPED_BUDGET` backfill whose items still wait abandons them and closes their sessions, and ends it `CANCELLED`.
- **D4: a day with a `BACKFILL_BUDGET` item is not complete.** Such a day stays `PENDING` (it replaces the plan's Rule 33 reading of `FAILED` as terminal for that code only). A later backfill can fill it, as the previous point says. Amendment 4 lets a retry read the range again, which is how such an item is filled.
- **D5: the dry run's estimate counts mints, not buys.** The median `requests_used` of captured items, rounded up, times the distinct passing mints that a capturing backfill would capture: those with no item yet, or whose item it would take over. `buysPassing` stays a separate count.

Fixes that follow from these without a new decision. A backfill that replaces a live item rebuilds the live session it leaves from the items left: split at idle or day gaps while `OPEN`, only its span and first item recomputed (or deleted when empty) while `CLOSING` or `CLOSED`, never reopened. A watermark stored without `covered_from` gets the estimate the reference SQL makes (the wallet's earliest session creation, else the row's update time, less `initial-lookback`) on the next scan. Each tick reads only the `CLOSING` sessions whose items are all settled, so a large backfill's waiting sessions cannot starve live sessions of their close. The sweep takes live items before a backfill's. A provider failure after pages were read keeps those pages and their requests as a paused tick. Actions without a block time are counted on the backfill (`skippedNoBlockTime`). A repeated create returns the stored backfill even while automation is off. A day is never read from a truncated list of sessions. A backfill's sessions can sit beside a live session of the same night where `covered_from` falls inside it; verdicts are per trading day, so this is cosmetic.

Consequences: `automation_backfills.scanned_to` and `skipped_no_block_time` are additive (`docs/schema/V20261103120000__automation_backfill.sql`); the backfill responses gain `scannedTo` and `skippedNoBlockTime`; `/sessions` accepts the removed `sort=status` as an alias of the default order until the console is updated; `spring.task.scheduling.pool.size` is pinned to 1 in `application.yml`.

## Amendment 2026-11-03 (4): retrying stopped ranges and filtered backfills

Decided with the user on 2026-11-03, answering the two open points of amendment 3. It amends Key Decision 6 and the plan's Rules 32 and 35.

- **Coverage and the live floor are two things.** Coverage says whether a day's buys were discovered: live discovery from `covered_from` to its last complete scan, plus the range every ended unfiltered capturing backfill read (from `scanned_to`, or its range start once it finished, to its range end). The ranges are merged, so none is counted twice. `covered_from` is the live floor, where coverage is contiguous from the live start, and the bound a new capturing backfill is clipped to. Only a `DONE` unfiltered backfill moves it. Amendment 3 let a stopped or cancelled backfill move it; that is withdrawn, because it made the range they read unreachable for a retry.
- **A retry may read a stopped or cancelled range again.** A new capturing backfill is clipped to the floor and blocked only by an active backfill and a `DONE` unfiltered one. It re-reads the whole range and takes over what the stopped backfills left that produced nothing (untouched, `BACKFILL_BUDGET`, abandoned before a dataset), so the `PENDING` days they left can complete. The cost is re-reading pages, which the retry's own budget bounds.
- **A filtered backfill never counts as coverage.** A backfill with a token list or a minimum signal above `capture-min-signal-sol` is `filtered` (recorded at creation, `automation_backfills.filtered`). It captures its buys, but buys the capture filter would take produced no item, so its range is not coverage and does not move the floor; its days stay `PENDING` until live coverage or an unfiltered backfill covers them. It blocks no one, and an unfiltered backfill may take over its untouched and budget-failed items. A filtered backfill may overlap a `DONE` filtered one (re-finding a buy is harmless, since a mint has one item) and may not overlap an unfiltered `DONE` or an active backfill.
- **A cancel or manual close abandons the pending items of a `CLOSED` session** (the phase 0 backlog) too; the session keeps its state and reason.

Consequences: `automation_backfills.filtered` is additive (`docs/schema/V20261103120000__automation_backfill.sql`); backfill responses gain `filtered`.

## Revisit when

- Observation rows pass about 50 million, or the dashboard's p95 stays above 1 s after indexing: consider a columnar store.
- A second consumer of the buy or run events appears: consider a broker.
- Unscored items exceed about 20% in practice: consider blocking or flagging such evaluations.
- Automation moves to a server or more than one instance: add a lease or leader election before capture starts.
